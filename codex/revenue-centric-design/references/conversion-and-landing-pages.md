## Performance is conversion: 31% better LCP → +8% sales (Vodafone A/B)

**Apply when:** founders separate "conversion work" from performance work.

- Treat web performance as a conversion lever, not an engineering nicety. Raise conversion before you open Figma.
- Test LCP and web vitals as rigorously as copy, first on paid traffic (every bounce is paid for).

**Evidence:** Vodafone Italy A/B test of web-vitals optimization: LCP 31% better than control; same offer, creative, and screen; 100k clicks and 34k visits/day per variant, paid traffic; +8% sales, +15% lead-to-visit, +11% cart-to-visit.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-08-04](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2084589988843299217)

## Compliments aren't validation — "what's missing for you to PIX now?" is

**Apply when:** everyone loves your idea, nobody bought.

- Treat compliments as free and worthless. Validate by selling before building: do 20 sales this way.
- When you cannot close inside your ICP, ask: "Falta o que pra você fazer o PIX agora?" ("What's missing for you to send the PIX instant payment right now?")
- Read the pause. It shows the real objection (your build list) or excuses (the "loved it" was just politeness).
- Ask the follow-up: "If it had that, would you pay now?"
- Turn each doubt into a sales argument or an FAQ item.
- Design after, to scale what conversation proved sells.

**Evidence:** Richard sold 20 subscriptions off a gray clickable wireframe with a fake QR code. Nobody said "paint the wireframe". Buyers asked for invoices and a second mechanic login. The gray proved people with the pain, in a hurry to pay.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-07-07](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2074539418266812580) and [@richardrx · 2026-06-24](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2069743203863962080)

## "Paint the button" is judging the last line of a long cascade

**Apply when:** you gather or act on LP feedback ("make it more colorful"), or reach for button color first.

- Work the cascade in this order, visual layer last: ICP → the buyer's **awareness level** (Eugene Schwartz's 5 stages) → what you say and how → visual positioning (tone → form, color, type, space).
- At low awareness, open on the problem, name the pain in the first fold, then show the mechanism and your fix.
- At higher awareness, go straight to your advantages vs. alternatives.
- Never jump to button color. You silently (and probably wrongly) answer who you sell to, their awareness, which pain, and what tone.

**Quote:** "They're looking at the end of a process and thinking it's the beginning."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-06-23](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2069469303464730988)

## Scaling cold traffic is an honesty test for your page

**Apply when:** ad spend goes up but revenue barely moves, or the conversion rate "drops" although you fixed nothing.

- Treat conversion on warm audiences (existing followers) as inflated, not a performance number.
- Read the collapse after you scale into cold traffic as the page's real ceiling.
- Fix the obvious page defects first. Judge media after.

**Evidence:** A founder tripled ad spend. Revenue rose only 11%. Warm-traffic conversion of ~3% (driven by Instagram followers) cratered to 0.5% on cold traffic. Fixing the obvious lifted it to 2% — 4x the real number.

**Quote:** "Scaling budget into cold traffic works like an honesty test: it shows how much of your conversion is your page and how much is borrowed trust."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-06-08](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2063972594223661127)

## Test hero comprehension with a stranger from your ICP

**Apply when:** a hero trades clarity for "creativity". "Revolutionize your workflow" does not say what it does. "The definitive productivity platform" does not say for whom.

- Show your LP hero to one stranger from your ICP for 5 seconds, take it away, and ask "What does this product do? For whom?"
- Fail the hero if they cannot answer, however pretty it is.
- Expect three questions, almost in order: what is this → is this for me → why should I care now.
- Cut decoding effort (cognitive load). Pick clarity over cleverness at the top of the funnel.
- Build the hero from five elements:
  1. A headline saying what the product does, in the customer's language, not internal jargon.
  2. A subheadline nailing the ICP and the concrete result.
  3. Real visual proof: a screenshot of the product working, not a generic smiling illustration.
  4. A specific CTA.
  5. Immediate social proof (a logo or a number) above the fold.
- Write specific heroes: "Controle de comandas pra oficina mecânica" ("Work-order control for auto repair shops") beats "Software de gestão" ("Management software").
- Read with "Pass the 5-second test — lead with the problem, not the feature" (2026-05-01): that post argues for a pain-led headline, this one for a what-it-does headline — both demand the what / for whom / why-now answers inside 5 seconds.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-29](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2060409264683044995)

## Write the CTA microcopy, not the button color

**Apply when:** you A/B test button colors for weeks over a 0.3% (non-significant) delta while the label still says "Sign up" with no click trigger.

- Write the CTA label before you touch button color or shape. The label moves conversion far more: it reduces the mental effort of simulating what happens next.
- Answer the three questions the brain asks before clicking: (1) what happens when I click, (2) how long it takes, (3) what it costs/commits.
- Never use "Sign up". It answers none. "Start free in 30s" answers two.
- Add a click trigger (the small line under the CTA) to answer the rest and break an objection: "Start Free Trial" + "14 days, no card".
- Root the copy in outcome bias and Construal Level Theory (making the outcome concrete).

**Quote:** "The button is the last thing the user reads before deciding — treat it as such."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-25](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2058875777739866490)

## Cut CAC by filtering on the landing page, not the media

**Apply when:** leads reach SDR calls but calls don't close, and founders swap creative or channel.

- Treat high CAC as a filtering problem first, rarely a media problem: qualify leads on the landing page before the form.
- Pull three filtering levers so the lead self-selects without feeling filtered:
  1. Copy specificity: "Cash flow for service providers billing R$500k–R$3M" beats "Organize your finances".
  2. Visible pricing: it screens out no-budget leads, so sales only meets real objections.
  3. A qualification question as the first interaction (or copy/examples that play that role).
- Measure CAC as cost per closed deal, not cost per lead.

**Evidence:** If you close 1 in 50 leads, dropping to 30 leads with 1 close nearly doubles media efficiency — same spend, lower CAC.

**Quote:** "The lead with no money won't magically generate money between the landing page and the sales call."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-18](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2056385204785213446)

## Aim for 4.2–4.5 stars, not a perfect 5

**Apply when:** you build reviews, ratings, or social proof.

- Never show a flawless rating. It reads as fake, and consumers distrust unanimity.
- Include constructive criticism in the profile. It feels more authentic and converts better than pure praise.
- Stack the highest-converting combination: a detailed case study with quantifiable ROI, ideally a video testimonial from a brand recognizable to your ICP, plus customer logos.
- Ban social proof done badly: identical cards, first-name-only, all 5 stars, generic avatars instead of a real photo.
- Make each review verifiable (e.g., link to the actual LinkedIn post).

**Evidence:** Northwestern: purchase probability peaks at 4.2–4.5 stars, not 5. Testimonials lift LP conversion up to 34% (VWO). 5+ reviews → 270% more likely to be bought (Yotpo). 93% read reviews before buying (BrightLocal).

**Visual:** A social-proof wall done right: real faces, names + roles, verifiable reviews, and recognizable client logos (Volkswagen, Coca-Cola, Samsung, Itaú, Volvo, DocuSign, Hotmart) under a "+10,000 professionals" headline — `../assets/2054910531132183030__1.jpg`

**Quote:** "Credibility drives conversion."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-14](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2054910531132183030)

## Compress the decision window — shorten the path to action below the path to doubt

**Apply when:** you optimize the checkout, CTA, or headline while you ignore the deliberation time upstream.

- Assume the purchase decision is mostly made before checkout. Make the path to the action shorter than the path to doubt.
- Compress decision time in one of two ways:
  1. Pressurize the environment with real scarcity ("Only 1 room left at this price", "Booked 3 times in the last hour"). It works through loss aversion (Kahneman: losing hurts up to ~2x more than the equivalent gain), so "1 left" registers as "I'll lose this".
  2. Remove steps entirely (Amazon's patented 1-Click ordering).

**Evidence:** Booking runs 1,000+ simultaneous experiments (per Lukas Vermeer, 8 yrs leading experimentation). Showing sold-out hotels alongside available ones *raised* bookings by sharpening perceived scarcity.

**Quote:** "The user converts when the path to the action is shorter than the path to doubt."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-11](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2053832356293738914)

## Don't clone the page — the converting layer is invisible

**Apply when:** you are tempted to clone a page that "looks like it works".

- Never copy the layout. It copies the cheap part and leaves the invisible research behind.
- Ask before copying: which parts are a function of the *product*, and which of the *customer research* behind each line and element?
- Look for the hidden layer: customer vocabulary pulled from interviews; objections ordered to the ICP's specific fear; social proof hand-picked to resonate; a core promise tuned to the stated desire.
- Name the error: cargo cult (replicating the visible ritual without grasping the causal mechanism).

**Evidence:** Two ~95%-identical pages, same niche/offer/order: 4% (Richard's client) vs 0.6% (a near-pixel clone by a builder).

**Quote:** "There's a name for copying the form expecting the function. It's called cargo cult."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-09](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2053086365781205142)

## Pass the 5-second test — lead with the problem, not the feature

**Apply when:** your hero opens by describing what the product *is/does* (a feature/category) instead of the visitor's pain.

- Assume a visitor scans your LP for ~5 seconds. If they cannot say what it does, who it's for, and why they should care, they leave.
- Open with the visitor's problem, not yourself. Replace the first line, as in this pair:
  - Before: "AI-powered project management platform" → meh (it talks about itself)
  - After: "Your team loses 6 hours a week hunting for information" → keep going (it talks about me)
- Connect the first line with the pain. If it doesn't, the rest of the page goes invisible.

**Quote:** "If you who built it can't answer in 5 seconds, your visitor can't either."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-05-01](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2050280273682510230)

## Fix contrast, layout, and trust before rewriting copy

**Apply when:** you rewrite home copy for weeks chasing conversion without touching the visual/attentional layer.

- Work three pre-verbal variables before you rewrite copy: contrast, placement, and trust. The brain processes them before the words, so they usually outweigh any headline.
- Contrast: set it relative to surroundings, not as an isolated color. A big, differently-shaped button in whitespace is *seen before it's read*.
- Contrast: treat the accessibility ratio 4.5:1 as a floor, not the goal.
- Placement: apply Fitts's law (closer, bigger targets get clicked more) and the F-pattern (Nielsen Norman eyetracking).
- Placement: never put a CTA bottom-right with no visual anchor. It sits outside the attentional map.
- Trust: build four reinforcing layers, and align all four. If one fails, the user invents a rational reason to leave.
  1. A real (non-stock) human face.
  2. A known brand or specific verifiable proof.
  3. An offer that fits the stated problem.
  4. A solution mechanism logically coherent with the promise.

**Quote:** "Optimizing copy without working contrast, placement, and trust is masking the symptom."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-04-28](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2049215050997784774)

## Specificity is the difference between decoration and persuasion

**Apply when:** your hero reads "The complete platform for [generic category]", a 5-feature subtitle, "Start free", and no real social proof — the default Brazilian SaaS template.

- Write specific copy. The brain discards generic LP copy.
- Show the *transformation* the product causes, not just what it does.
- Fix the four standard failures:
  1. "Complete platform" means nothing → "Cut new-dev onboarding time 40%"
  2. Features on top → lead with the pain, feature as the solution (nobody wakes up wanting a "custom report feature")
  3. "Start free" is the weakest CTA (no value, no risk reduction, no urgency) → "See your first report in 2 minutes"
  4. "Used by Company X" proves nothing → "We cut Company X's churn from 12% to 6%"

**Evidence:** Across 30 Brazilian SaaS LPs: descriptive vs transformation-led ran 0.5% vs 3% conversion — same traffic, 6x more leads — driven by argument sequence and promise specificity, not visual design.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-04-17](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2045154631974539650)

## Users scan, they don't read — give the primary action obvious contrast

**Apply when:** the main CTA competes with 6 other elements, or the key action is buried inside a click-to-reveal dropdown.

- Design for scanning: users ignore most of the interface and enter with one question, "What can I do here?"
- Make the primary action visually dominant, so it wins on contrast within seconds. Never let it compete with too many elements or hide behind interaction: it loses prominence or becomes invisible.
- Leverage image superiority to guide attention down the page.

**Visual:** A login screen annotated with two biases — "limited choice bias" (a single dominant Google sign-in button) and "image superiority" (a vivid hero illustration pulling the eye) — `../assets/2042013751742705816__1.jpg`

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-04-08](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2042013751742705816)

## Judge a landing page by conversion, not by beauty

**Apply when:** debates over aesthetics ("AI vs human", "who designed it better") ignore conversion.

- Never treat a landing page as a beauty contest. Judge it by its one measurable job: qualify the user and lower CAC by turning traffic into revenue.
- Never evaluate a static image with no context. It is nearly useless.
- Treat conversion as a continuous loop, never an isolated event: form a thesis → ship the interface and collect data → find where the user hesitated → form a new hypothesis → optimize the next cycle.

**Visual:** A side-by-side "Google Stitch vs Human" LP comparison judged on looks alone (context, not a model to copy) — `../assets/2034638793219694734__q__1.jpg`

**Quote:** "Product design doesn't compete with art. It sells."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-03-19](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2034638793219694734)

## Converting pages are often ugly — design for performance, not applause

**Apply when:** you choose between a page that wins design compliments and one built to convert.

- Expect the converting page to be visually aggressive and text-heavy, far from the Apple aesthetic. Branding (looking good in the screenshot) fights performance (conversion, retention, expansion).
- Engineer the converting pattern:
  1. One obvious attention point: eyes go where they must.
  2. A high-contrast CTA with redundancy.
  3. A clear promise delivering easily-perceived value.
  4. Guarantee, social proof, and a free element to reduce risk aversion.
  5. Organic images by the second fold to back the offer and build trust.

**Evidence:** The analyzed page (quoted): 100 visits → 19 signups, a 19% conversion rate.

**Visual:** An "ugly-but-converts" waterproofing LP: urgency bar, high-contrast orange CTAs, a short above-the-fold form, stat row (12,500+ / 4.9★ / 20+ yrs / 100%), and a trust-badge strip (incl. lifetime warranty) — `../assets/2023777916673220817__q__1.jpg`

**Quote:** "Some LPs go after compliments, others go after conversion."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-02-17](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2023777916673220817)

## Design the features page for skimming, not reading

**Apply when:** you pack a features page with dense prose.

- Design for skimming: visitors skim three bullets and hunt for a demo video. Front-load three crisp bullets and make a demo video easy to find.
- Write the deep copy for the minority of technical, high-awareness buyers who *will* read the detail.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-02-09](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2020815036168437814)

## No clicks out, plus brutal CTA contrast

**Apply when:** you are tempted to link out to a glowing NY Times piece or a top influencer's video, or your CTA blends into the interface.

- Keep distraction low and redundancy high. Treat any clickable element that leads off-page as lost conversion, however prestigious the source: kill outbound clicks entirely.
- Make the CTA contrast strongly with the rest of the interface in position, size, and color. A CTA that is hard to notice and click will not be clicked.

**Visual:** A wireframe of the contrast a CTA button needs against the surrounding interface — `../assets/2019514730696565238__1.jpg`

## Debug ICP and awareness level before touching design

**Apply when:** your LP underperforms and you reach for button-color tweaks, or you define ICP by demographics.

- Treat conversion as context engineering: who arrives, with what pain, at what stage, needing what proof.
- Debug two invisible variables first: the Who (ICP) and the When (awareness level).
- Reject the demographic fallacy. Geography, age, and income are a bad proxy: King Charles and Ozzy Osbourne are demographic twins with opposite needs.
- Define the real ICP by *buying criteria*: trigger, pain, prior attempt, proof needed. Persona is only biography, the spec for your user.
- Map the visitor's Eugene Schwartz awareness level. Write the headline to the current stage; use proof to push one stage forward.
- Never sell "Solution" to the "Unaware". It is proposing on a first date.
- Debug in this order: ICP wrong → awareness wrong → proof insufficient for the stage → only then touch visual design.
- Use this table for awareness → angle / hero / proof:

|Awareness|Angle|Hero|Proof|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Unaware**|symptom & identity|"Still doing X this way?"|simple diagnostic, checklist, benchmark|
|**Problem-aware**|cost & urgency|"If you have X, you're losing Y"|numbers, before/after, calculation|
|**Solution-aware**|trade-offs & selection|"3 ways to solve X — why the 3rd scales"|honest comparison, matrix|
|**Product-aware**|differentiation & proof stack|"Why us, why now"|cases, demo, objections|
|**Most aware**|final risk & friction|"Swap X for Y in Z days, no risk"|guarantees, onboarding, effort reduction|

**Visual:**

- The demographic-twins diagram (Charles vs Ozzy, identical on paper) — `../assets/2011427153133351046__2.jpg`

**Quote:** "There's no 'conversion rate' in a vacuum. There's contextualized conversion."

## The 2026 landing page checklist

**Apply when:** you audit or build any LP.

- Run this checklist from @namyakhann (Supafast), which Richard translated and endorsed in full, top to bottom.
- Hero (5): state 1 clear promise; target 1 specific audience; use 1 exact-action CTA; show, don't explain, in the visual; place a trust seal above the fold.
- Copy (5): write a headline <10 words; put benefit before feature; use customer language; sell one central transformation; make the page scannable in 5s.
- Social proof (4): show logos if you have names; show metrics if you have traction; use specific result testimonials; use zero generic praise.
- Structure (4): order sections Hero → Pain → Solution → Proof → CTA; set max 3 feature sections; write the FAQ to kill top 5 objections; repeat the CTA 2–3x.
- Technical (3): load in <3s; build real mobile-first; keep a single-scroll flow.

**Mantra:** Clarity > creativity. Result > feature. Speed > polish. If it doesn't convert in 2026, it's not your traffic — it's your page.

**Conflict note (headline length):** The Supafast hero formula in [ai-era-differentiation.md](ai-era-differentiation.md) says `Headline (≤8 words, attack the #1 pain)`. This checklist says headline <10 words. Apply the stricter rule: keep the headline at 8 words or fewer.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2026-01-02](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2007216871607771303)

## Click triggers: one line of risk-reducing microcopy next to the CTA

**Apply when:** users hesitate right before the CTA.

- Add a single line of text next to the CTA that reduces perceived risk. CRO calls it a "click trigger"; marketing calls it objection-breaking.
- Write click triggers like these: "Lifetime guarantee," "Free shipping," "7 days free, no card."
- Find the real objections by talking to customers and reading complaint pages (ReclameAqui) and app-store comments. Test which click trigger performs best.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2025-12-19](https://x.com/richardrx/status/2002076748112875558)

## Founder LP guide: platform doesn't matter, load time does; proof must outweigh the promise

**Apply when:** you build a first LP cheaply.

- Rank speed, clarity, contrast, proof, and direct action above the platform: profits are more sensitive to conversions than to costs.
- Follow five rules:
  1. Use any platform, but deliver content fast and target an "A" on GTMetrix.
  2. Lead with a short pain or clear benefit, then make the promise tangible in the subhead.
  3. Create visual contrast with what the ICP sees daily.
  4. Ensure proof ≥ promise, moving from strong/shallow at the top to denser evidence below.
  5. Use direct CTAs tied to the outcome about every 1.5 sections.

**Quote:** "Nobody wants to know about your product's features — they want what it generates or the pain it kills. Not 6GB of space, 3,000 songs in your pocket."

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2025-11-10](https://x.com/richardrx/status/1987842649261261240)

## Match CTA microcopy to the funnel stage

**Apply when:** one generic CTA label serves the whole journey.

- Change button text with funnel progress. Match the verb to the mental state at each stage.
- Use these labels:
  - First interaction: "Descubra como funciona" ("See how it works")
  - Product page: "Quero esse produto" ("I want this product")
  - Checkout: "Finalizar minha compra" ("Complete my purchase")
- Keep each label short (≤4 words), clear, and directional. This raises perceived control, confidence, and conversion.
- Audit every CTA against the stage it appears in. Test stage-matched variants.

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2025-11-07](https://x.com/richardrx/status/1986836679953719632)

## Real humans outconvert models — banner blindness explains why

**Apply when:** you choose imagery for LPs and proof sections.

- Prefer real users, the real team, user-generated reviews, and simple real photos over polished models and studio shots. They often win.
- Likely explanation: banner blindness. People learn to ignore what looks like advertising and give attention to what is organic or unusual.
- Chinese sites exploit this at scale with gamified UGC.

**Evidence:** Highrise, the 37signals CRM (later Basecamp), tested homepage variants with different human figures. Real users and actual team developers converted best on average, above stock-photo models (the post's comparison). 37signals' own writeup ([Signal v. Noise, "Behind the scenes: A/B testing part 3"](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/2991-behind-the-scenes-ab-testing-part-3-final)) photographed only real Highrise customers, no stock models, no staff. The specific person barely mattered: the lift came from the person-photo layout beating the long-form page.

**Visual:** The Highrise homepage test side by side — the illustrated "original design" vs the "person design" fronted by a real customer's photo and quote, marked +102.5% — `../assets/1985681720104903142__1.jpg`

**Source:** [@richardrx · 2025-11-04](https://x.com/richardrx/status/1985681720104903142)
